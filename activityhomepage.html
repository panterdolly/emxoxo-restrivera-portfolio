Overview

Too many steps before the real learning begins

This project focused on improving the current journey of the BSA1 reading activity — one of the core activity types within Voila, Cambridge UP&A's English learning platform for B1–B2 secondary learners.

As Lead UI/UX Designer, I led the full exploration and redesign — from identifying drop-off patterns in the existing flow to delivering a new activity experience that was tested with real learners in Cambridge partner schools in Brazil.

  • Identified a critical drop-off pattern caused by excessive pre-screens before learners reached the actual activity
  • Redesigned the BSA1 activity flow to be faster, cleaner, and more engaging
  • Delivered measurable outcomes — increased activity completion rates and faster average task time

Empathise

Users were dropping off before they even started

The research phase centred on a key question: what are the major pain points learners encounter while using our activities?

Through analysis of the existing live activity flow, the answer became clear: learners were required to cross a series of pre-screens before reaching the actual content. Each screen required an action — and the cumulative effect of these steps was fatigue, frustration, and drop-off after completing just one activity.

Users are dropping off after completing only one activity — forced to navigate multiple pre-screens before accessing live content makes the current process tiring and inefficient.

The feedback system within the existing BSA1 activity also contained very basic information, providing little value to the learner and offering no incentive to continue. Both issues needed to be addressed together.

Opportunity

Remove the friction — keep the learning

By optimising the flow and creating a more engaging experience, learners would be able to move through activities faster — spending more time learning and less time navigating. This would also generate more data for the platform's calibration engine, creating a better experience over time.

Streamline the pre-activity flow

Eliminate unnecessary screens between the activity selection and the actual content — reducing the number of required actions and lowering the cognitive load before learning even begins.

Improve feedback quality

Upgrade the feedback system from basic information to meaningful, actionable responses — giving learners a reason to reflect, continue, and return to the platform.

Drive more completions

A faster, more engaging flow directly increases the number of activities completed per session — generating richer data for the calibration team and better outcomes for learners.

User personas

Two learners with one shared frustration

The same two personas that surfaced across the Voila platform research were central to this project — both experiencing the same core problem: too much friction before reaching the actual learning content.

SD

Sally Doe

Student, 11 · Full-time student

A bookworm and active English language learner. Sally uses YouTube and reading to build her skills — but gets frustrated when platforms put too many steps between her and the content she came for.

Goals
  • Access optimised, straightforward learning material with clear feedback
  • Get to reading and listening activities without unnecessary obstacles
Frustrations
  • Too long a process before reaching the actual activity
  • Pre-screens feel like obstacles rather than preparation
MD

Milan Dimitri

Student, 18 · Full-time student

A fan of fan fiction and gaming, Milan expects digital interfaces to be intuitive and rewarding. He gets frustrated when UI elements are confusing or when feedback doesn't feel accurate or meaningful.

Goals
  • Straightforward learning with clear, accurate feedback and intuitive icons
  • An experience that respects his time and keeps him engaged
Frustrations
  • Complex and confusing UI elements break his flow
  • Dull, generic feedback with confusing iconography

Ideation

Designing for speed, clarity, and continuity

The ideation phase was grounded in three How Might We questions — each addressing a different dimension of the drop-off problem:

How might we improve the user experience within the bitesize activities to reduce fatigue and drop-off?
How might we increase the average number of activities taken by each learner to gather more calibration data?
How might we make feedback more meaningful so learners feel motivated to continue?

All design decisions were made within the Cambridge One Design System and branding guidelines. Alignment with the development team was crucial — many of the new components, flows, and interactions required feasibility validation before committing to the hi-fidelity direction.

Reflections

Communication and alignment are as important as the design itself

This project was successfully implemented — and I'm grateful for the support of my Scrum team and the trust they placed in me as lead designer during the exploration stage. Three key lessons stood out:

  • Communication is key. The project was fast-paced — we needed to complete it before the next quarterly planning cycle. Clear, frequent communication kept everyone aligned under pressure
  • Alignment is crucial. Since most components, flows, and interactions were new, I consulted closely with developers to validate feasibility before committing to hi-fidelity. This saved significant rework time
  • Design system advocacy matters. Convincing the design team about new colour choices and interaction patterns required me to clearly articulate the rationale — ensuring no design system rules were violated while still pushing the experience forward
  • Data is a design outcome. Increasing activity completion wasn't just a UX win — it directly improved the calibration data the platform needed to personalise learning. Designing for engagement had a measurable downstream business impact
