Overview

Great ideas fail without the right team — Series fixes that

Series is a concept app for startup aspirants to find co-founders, protect their ideas, and build together. As Lead Designer and Project Manager, I led the end-to-end design across three prototype iterations and a final usability test.

  • Identified a gap no existing platform addressed: purpose-built co-founder matching
  • Introduced an in-app NDA signing flow — directly solving users' fear of idea theft
  • Designed for three distinct user archetypes with very different comfort levels and goals

Empathise

The tools exist — but none were built for this

Through competitive analysis and user interviews, two pain points emerged consistently: finding goal-aligned collaborators and fear of sharing ideas online. LinkedIn was the most cited platform — but it's transactional, not collaborative. Posts seeking co-founders got buried, and the back-and-forth to establish trust was exhausting.

Startup aspirants can't find like-minded collaborators through existing platforms — and fear of idea theft makes them reluctant to share openly.

This dual problem — discoverability and trust — became the design north star for every major decision in Series.

Identified opportunities

Three areas where Series could do what no existing platform does

After synthesis, I mapped the opportunity space and identified three areas where a purpose-built platform could create real differentiation:

Intent-first matching

Unlike LinkedIn, Series would surface people based on shared startup goals and industry interests — not job titles. This shifts the mental model from "networking" to "building together."

Built-in trust infrastructure

An in-app NDA signing flow was a deliberate design choice to lower the emotional barrier to sharing ideas — giving users a structural safeguard before they open up about their concept.

Growth beyond matching

Series Hackathons and Mentorship Programs were designed as ecosystem features — giving users a reason to stay and grow on the platform, not just find a team and leave.

User personas

Three users, three very different barriers to collaboration

My research surfaced three distinct user archetypes — each with unique motivations and blockers. Designing for all three required balancing simplicity with depth, and accessibility with sophistication.

LD

Lara Doe

Student, 16 · Aspiring Builder

Curious and driven, Lara spends her free time on YouTube learning about startups. She has ideas but no peers who share her ambition — and most platforms exclude her due to age restrictions.

Goals
  • Find peers who share her interest in building something real
  • Use a platform that feels safe and age-inclusive
Frustrations
  • Most collaboration platforms are 18+ only
  • Social platforms feel noisy and unstructured
  • Fake posts and low-intent groups waste her time
AS

Alfred Sullyvan

CMO, 30 · Aspiring Co-Founder

A marketing professional with a clear business idea. He lacks a technical co-founder and is frustrated by the inefficiency of cross-posting across platforms to find serious candidates.

Goals
  • Find a reliable, goal-aligned technical co-founder efficiently
  • Reach serious aspirants through a single, purpose-built channel
Frustrations
  • Cross-platform posting yields low-quality responses
  • Hard to gauge seriousness and commitment of potential collaborators
DL

David Lee

Entrepreneur, 50 · Veteran Builder

David has built businesses before but needs a partner who understands modern technology. He knows what he wants but doesn't know where to look — and existing platforms feel too complex.

Goals
  • Find a co-founder with modern tech and digital expertise
  • Use a platform that doesn't require tech-savviness to navigate
Frustrations
  • Existing platforms feel complex and hard to navigate
  • Worries about putting his ideas online without protection

Ideation

The NDA wasn't an afterthought — it was the key design decision

No existing platform addressed idea protection directly. I saw an opportunity to bake trust into the product architecture itself — making the NDA a confidence signal, not a legal add-on. This single feature meaningfully separated Series from every competitor.

I led HMW workshops that pushed the team beyond surface-level solutions:

How might we match aspirants based on shared goals — not just job titles or industries?
How might we give users enough confidence to share their real idea with a stranger?
How might we protect users' concepts structurally — so trust is designed in, not assumed?

Usability testing

Validating the collaboration journey — including the hard parts

I ran unmoderated usability testing through Maze with participants spanning all three persona archetypes. The test scenario was designed to simulate the full journey from first-time signup to NDA signing.

Scenario: You have an idea that could solve other people's problems. As a first-time Series user, show how you would build your team to kick off your project.

The 70% NDA completion rate was the most instructive result — some participants didn't realise the NDA step was optional at the early stage. This informed a post-testing fix: adding a clearer contextual prompt explaining when and why the NDA gets triggered.

Reflections

What I'd take forward — and what I'd revisit

Series was one of the most complex challenges I've tackled — not because of the UI, but because of the human dynamics it was solving. Trust is hard to design for.

  • The 70% NDA completion rate revealed a discoverability issue — I added a contextual prompt post-testing explaining when and why the NDA gets triggered
  • I'd test the NDA concept at lo-fi stage next time — validating comprehension earlier would have saved iteration cycles
  • David Lee's persona was under-served in V3 — accessibility of the core flows needs more attention in the next iteration

This project reinforced that the best design decisions aren't always the obvious ones — the NDA came from listening carefully to what users were actually afraid of, then designing directly for that fear.
