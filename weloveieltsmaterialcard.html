Overview

Making study materials easier to read — for every type of learner

We Love IELTS is an English platform developed by Cambridge University Press & Assessment for IELTS takers. It provides test guidance and study tips from IELTS experts, teachers, and students through lessons, podcasts, videos, and quizzes.

As UI/UX Designer, I was tasked with redesigning the WLI material card — a core UI component used across the platform to surface learning content. The card was cluttered, hard to read, and creating friction for users trying to find what they needed.

  • Identified readability and clutter as the core usability problem with the existing card
  • Redesigned the component within the Cambridge One Design System and branding guidelines
  • Delivered a cleaner, more accessible card that received positive feedback from stakeholders and users

Empathise

Almost unreadable — and users noticed

The research phase centred on a single critical question: what challenges do users face when reading the text on the WLI material card?

The answer was clear and consistent: the existing card was almost unreadable and too busy. Thumbnail text was overlapping with other text elements, creating a visually cluttered environment that made it hard for learners to quickly identify and access the content they needed.

Users struggle to read WLI material cards due to poor readability and excessive clutter — overlapping thumbnail text creates a busy, nearly unreadable visual environment.

This wasn't just an aesthetic issue. For IELTS learners — many of whom are already managing the pressure of exam preparation — a confusing UI adds unnecessary friction to an already demanding process.

Opportunity

A cleaner card means better learning outcomes

The opportunity was clear: redesign the WLI material card to enhance readability and reduce clutter. By creating a cleaner, more user-friendly interface, learners would be able to easily access and understand the information — significantly improving their overall experience and engagement with the material.

Improve readability

Eliminate overlapping text and create clear visual hierarchy so users can instantly understand what a card contains without straining.

Reduce visual clutter

Simplify the card layout by establishing consistent placement rules for type tags, thumbnails, and metadata — reducing cognitive load.

Inclusive design

Apply colour contrast, font sizing, and layout improvements that make the card accessible to a wider range of learners — regardless of device or ability.

User personas

Two very different learners, one shared frustration

Despite their different backgrounds and motivations, both personas experienced the same core problem: the card was hard to read and made finding the right material frustrating.

ND

Nate Doe

Student, 18 · Full-time secondary student

A goal-oriented full-time student preparing for IELTS and upcoming university entrance exams. Actively seeks resources to optimise his study experience and needs to find materials quickly and efficiently.

Goals
  • Study English and easily find materials for daily practice
  • Access the right resources without wasting time navigating
Frustrations
  • Material cards are almost unreadable and visually busy
  • Inconsistent placement of type tags makes scanning difficult
JD

Janu Deli

Working student, 22 · Health worker

A graduate and devoted health worker preparing for IELTS to pursue opportunities abroad. Balancing a demanding career with language study, she needs a platform that respects her limited time.

Goals
  • Find study materials quickly during short breaks between shifts
  • Improve English proficiency efficiently without unnecessary friction
Frustrations
  • Has a hard time reading some of the material cards
  • Limited time means clutter and confusion are especially costly

Ideation

Designing for comprehension, not just aesthetics

The ideation phase was guided by How Might We questions that pushed beyond surface-level visual fixes — encouraging the team to consider the full range of accessibility and usability improvements that could make the card genuinely easier to use.

How might we enhance user comprehension and engagement with the WLI material cards?
How might we make it fast and easy for learners to find the material they need?

All design decisions were made within the Cambridge One Design System and branding guidelines — ensuring the redesigned card was consistent with the broader platform ecosystem.

Testing

Validated through Maze — published to production

The redesigned WLI material card underwent a series of usability tests through Maze and was successfully published on the We Love IELTS website. The new card received positive feedback from both stakeholders and users.

Planned updates for the post-MVP version were not implemented due to the temporary closure of the website — however the core redesign shipped and delivered measurable improvements in readability and user experience.

The redesigned card solved the core readability problem — stakeholders and users responded positively to the cleaner layout and improved visual hierarchy. Significant testing insights and synthesised data remain confidential per Cambridge UP&A guidelines.

Reflections

What this project reinforced about inclusive design

The most important lesson from this project was the significance of consistently prioritising inclusive design to cater to a broad spectrum of users.

  • A single UI component — when poorly designed — can create friction for every user on the platform, regardless of their tech literacy
  • Working within an established design system (Cambridge One) sharpened my ability to solve problems creatively within constraints
  • Stakeholder and user alignment early in the process made the final delivery smoother and the feedback more positive
  • The post-MVP features not being shipped was a reminder that shipping the right thing matters more than shipping everything
